How much sunlight will reach Singapore in the next 3 hours?

This site forecasts solar irradiance, the sunlight reaching the ground, over Singapore 1, 2 and 3 hours ahead. It combines live Himawari satellite images with the last 24 hours of weather.

Solar panel output follows sunlight almost exactly, and clear-sky sunlight is predictable astronomy. So the hard part is cloud, and a satellite can see cloud upwind before it reaches the island.

From satellite to forecast

  1. 1 · InputsLive weather (24 h)3 satellite frames, 10 min apart
  2. 2 · ModelImage encoder + weather sequence model, fused
  3. 3 · CloudinessClear-sky index kt × clear-sky sunlight
  4. 4 · OutputForecast + 90% range

1 · Inputs

Every run downloads the last 24 hours of weather for Singapore: cloud cover, temperature, humidity, rain, wind and recent sunlight. It also fetches the three most recent Himawari-9 satellite scans, which arrive every 10 minutes from NOAA's public archive.

2 · Model

An image network pretrained on ImageNet turns each satellite frame into features, and a sequence model reads the weather history. A cross-attention layer lets the weather side "look at" the relevant parts of the images. Three copies trained with different random seeds each produce a forecast, and their outputs are combined, giving more weight to the more confident ones.

3 · Cloudiness, not raw sunlight

The model doesn't predict sunlight directly. It predicts the clear-sky index (kt): the fraction of a cloudless sky's sunlight that gets through. 1.0 means clear sky and 0.3 means heavy cloud. Clear-sky sunlight comes from solar geometry, so the model can spend all its effort on cloud.

4 · Forecast with uncertainty

Multiplying kt by clear-sky sunlight gives the forecast in W/m². Each forecast also comes with a 90% interval: if the model is well calibrated, the real value should land inside that range about 9 times out of 10.

How forecasts are checked

Once a target hour has passed, the forecast is compared with the sunlight reported for that hour. It's also compared with smart persistence, a simple baseline that assumes the current cloudiness carries on unchanged. A forecast model is only useful if it beats that baseline.

Caveat: "actual" sunlight comes from the ERA5 reanalysis product, not a ground sensor. ERA5 is smoothed in space and time, so verification is a consistency check rather than independent ground truth. Training data covers 08:00–17:00 SGT only.
